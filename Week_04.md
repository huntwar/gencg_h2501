# Week 04

For my clock project, I wanted to go beyond the normal idea of a clock as something that just shows numbers and moving hands. I didn’t want it to only measure time — I wanted it to express it. To me, time doesn’t always feel strict and mechanical. It feels emotional, fluid, and constantly changing. So instead of using hands, I used color, shapes, and movement to show time passing like a mood or a breath — growing, shifting, and fading.

My clock doesn’t really “tell” time in the usual way. Instead, it turns each moment into something visual and meaningful. The colors change, the forms expand and disappear, and that makes time feel alive rather than fixed.

This project also made me think about how we experience time. A real clock moves forward in a straight line, but life often feels cyclical — days repeat, routines return, and patterns come back again and again. I tried to reflect that by making the motion loop, while still changing slowly inside each loop. That way, you can feel both repetition and progress at the same time.

Working on this helped me see that coding isn’t just technical — it can be expressive. I wasn’t just building a system to track time, I was creating a way to visualize how time feels to me: flowing, changing, and always coming back around.
